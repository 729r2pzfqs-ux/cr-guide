The two materials

PP

Polypropylene

Polypropylene resists most acids, alkalis and salt solutions. Concentrated oxidising acids, chlorinated solvents and aromatic hydrocarbons attack it, and it turns brittle below about 0 °C.

PMP

Polymethylpentene (TPX)

Polymethylpentene (TPX) is a transparent polyolefin used for labware. It resists many acids and alkalis, while aromatic and chlorinated solvents and strong oxidisers attack it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is PP or PMP more chemical-resistant?

Of 416 chemicals rated for both materials at 20 °C, PP and PMP share the same rating in 276 rows (66 %). PP is rated better in 111 rows and PMP in 29 rows.

How do PP and PMP compare for chemical resistance?

Based on 416 chemicals tested for both: PP is rated better with Vegetable and animal oils, fats, essential oils, Fuels, mineral oils and aliphatic hydrocarbons, Ketones and aldehydes, Amines and nitrogen compounds. PMP is rated better with –. They perform similarly with Mineral acids, Alkalis, Salts and salt solutions, Oxidising agents and halogens.

About this data

The ratings come from the chemical resistance list published by Bürkle GmbH, which compiled them from raw-material manufacturers’ data. They describe laboratory tests on the raw material at 20 °C and 50 °C.

A finished part also sees pressure, mechanical stress, welds and mixtures that a laboratory test does not. Treat the rating as a first filter and test under your own conditions before relying on it.
