Frequently Asked Questions

Which materials resist Phenol at high concentrations?

The materials with an A rating (resistant) for Phenol at 20 °C are: PTFE, PP, PVDF, SS 316, SS 304, Aluminium, FEP, ECTFE/ETFE. These can generally be used in direct contact.

Which materials should be avoided with Phenol?

Materials rated D (not resistant) for Phenol are: PVC Rigid, EPDM, NBR, Nylon (PA), Acetal (POM), Polycarbonate, Polystyrene, PETG. These should not be used in contact with Phenol.

Does temperature affect chemical resistance to Phenol?

Yes. 3 of 21 tested materials show a worse rating at 50 °C than at 20 °C for Phenol. Materials affected include: HDPE, PP, SS 304.

Is PTFE safe for Phenol?

PTFE is rated A for Phenol at 20 °C. A rating of A means resistant, B means limited resistance, C means partly resistant, and D means not resistant.

Does concentration affect chemical resistance to Phenol?

Yes. Phenol is tested at 2 concentrations (10 %, 100 %). Some materials change their rating depending on the concentration.

About this data

The ratings come from the chemical resistance list published by Bürkle GmbH, which compiled them from raw-material manufacturers’ data. They describe laboratory tests on the raw material at 20 °C and 50 °C.

A finished part also sees pressure, mechanical stress, welds and mixtures that a laboratory test does not. Treat the rating as a first filter and test under your own conditions before relying on it.
